\begin{abstract}
Hardware/software (HW/SW) partitioning is ultimately a schedule-sensitive problem: the best partition is not only the one with low isolated execution cost, but the one that interacts favorably with precedence constraints, software serialization, hardware concurrency, communication, and hardware-area limits. This paper focuses on the order-aware differentiable graph-neural implementation in our repository, namely \diffgnnorder (\texttt{diff\_gnn\_order}). The proposed method learns relaxed hardware assignments and software execution priorities jointly, optimizes a differentiable makespan surrogate, and then decodes the relaxed solution through feasibility repair and hybrid list-scheduling post-processing. The placement-only \diffgnn variant is treated as an ablation of the proposed model.

The experimental protocol is defined directly from the repository artifacts. Real benchmarks are TOSA task graphs stored under the \texttt{inputs} folder, with SqueezeNet-TOSA as the primary benchmark and additional PyTorch- and TFLite-derived graphs used for cross-graph evaluation. Each concrete task-graph instance is created by combining a fixed topology with sampled execution, area, and communication costs parameterized by hardware scale, variance, communication scale, and area budget. We evaluate partitions using DAG makespan, static-priority makespan, and learned-priority makespan; for non-order-aware baselines we report the static-priority result, while for \diffgnnorder we report the better of the learned and static schedules. To study scalability, we further introduce SqueezeNet-like synthetic DAGs with 1,000 and 10,000 nodes whose edge budget preserves the edge-to-node ratio of the original SqueezeNet-TOSA graph. The resulting manuscript provides a paper-ready experimental-settings and results template aligned with the ICCAD notation and the current implementation.
\end{abstract}
