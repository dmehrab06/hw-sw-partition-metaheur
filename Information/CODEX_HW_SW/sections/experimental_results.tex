\section{Experimental Settings}
\label{sec:settings}

\subsection{Datasets}
The real benchmark suite is stored under \texttt{inputs/task\_graph\_topology/soda-benchmark-graphs}. It contains seven TOSA task graphs: three PyTorch-derived graphs (\texttt{mobile\_net\_tosa.dot}, \texttt{rez\_net\_tosa.dot}, and \texttt{squeeze\_net\_tosa.dot}) and four TFLite-derived graphs (\texttt{anomaly\_detection\_tosa.dot}, \texttt{image\_classification\_tosa.dot}, \texttt{keyword\_spotting\_tosa.dot}, and \texttt{visual\_wake\_words\_tosa.dot}). Across these seven real DAGs, the benchmark range spans from 68 nodes and 63 edges up to 830 nodes and 1066 edges. SqueezeNet-TOSA remains the primary neural-network benchmark discussed throughout the paper, with 179 nodes and 234 directed edges. In addition to the SODA suite, we keep the custom sanity-check graph \texttt{inputs/task\_graph\_topology/custom/paper\_fig3\_11node.dot}, which contains 11 nodes and 12 edges and is used for controlled visualizations and ablations.

To study scalability beyond the real benchmarks, we generate SqueezeNet-like synthetic DAGs that preserve the edge-to-node ratio of the SqueezeNet template. In the current setup, we materialize synthetic graphs with $N\in\{11,50,1000,10000\}$ nodes, which produce 14, 65, 1307, and 13073 edges, respectively. Table~\ref{tab:graph_stats} summarizes the seven real SODA graphs together with the four synthetic SqueezeNet-like graphs used for scaling. In the makespan comparison, all non-order-aware baselines are reported by their discrete static-priority makespan $M_{\mathrm{static}}$, whereas \diffgnnorder reports both $M_{\mathrm{learned}}$ and
\begin{equation}
M_{\mathrm{report}}=\min(M_{\mathrm{static}},M_{\mathrm{learned}}).
\end{equation}
Concrete task-graph instances are created by calling \texttt{TaskGraph.load\_graph\_from\_pydot} on the real topologies or by loading the generated synthetic pickles under \texttt{inputs/task\_graph\_complete/}. The resulting instances are cached under \texttt{inputs/task\_graph\_complete/} using filenames of the form
\begin{center}
\texttt{taskgraph-squeeze\_net\_tosa-instance-config-<config>.pkl}.
\end{center}
The cached SqueezeNet sweep in the repository spans area ratios $\alpha \in \{0.1,0.3,0.5,0.7,0.9\}$, hardware scale factors $k \in \{0.1,0.3,0.5,0.7,0.9\}$, fixed variance $l=0.5$, fixed communication scale $\mu=1.0$, and multiple random seeds.

\subsection{Cost Model}
We use the cost-assignment mechanism implemented in \texttt{meta\_heuristic/task\_graph.py}. For every node $i$, the software execution cost is sampled as
\begin{equation}
t_i^{sw} \sim \mathcal{U}(1,100).
\end{equation}
The hardware execution cost is sampled from
\begin{equation}
t_i^{hw} = \max\!\left(0.1,\mathcal{N}\big(k \cdot t_i^{sw}, (l \cdot k \cdot t_i^{sw})^2\big)\right),
\end{equation}
where $k$ is the hardware scale factor and $l$ is the hardware-cost variance. Hardware area is sampled as
\begin{equation}
A_i \sim \mathcal{U}(1,A_{\max}),
\end{equation}
and the communication cost on edge $(j,i)$ is sampled as
\begin{equation}
C_{ji} \sim \mathcal{U}(0,2\mu s_{\max}),
\qquad
s_{\max}=\max_{v\in\gV} t_v^{sw},
\end{equation}
where $\mu$ is the communication scale factor. A partition is feasible only if
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i\in\gV} p_i A_i \le \alpha \sum_{i\in\gV} A_i.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Proposed-Method Settings}
Unless otherwise noted, the reported \diffgnnorder experiments use the order-aware configuration implemented in \texttt{diff\_gnn\_ordering.py}: a three-layer \gcn encoder with hidden dimension 256, dropout 0.2, \texttt{feature\_profile=default\_plus\_paper}, \texttt{edge\_weight\_mode=paper2\_cosine}, \texttt{edge\_weight\_learner=mlp}, 12 Sinkhorn iterations, two order-refinement steps, \texttt{pairwise\_mode=rank\_sigmoid}, pairwise temperature 0.35, no Gumbel noise, and \texttt{use\_hw\_ordering=False}. Training and final selection both use the queue-style differentiable metric, while the final discrete solution is refined with hybrid LSSP post-processing. On the small 11-node graph, we use the corresponding fast profile with hidden dimension 128 and 500 training iterations. The placement-only \diffgnn ablation uses the same encoder and post-processing pipeline but removes the ordering heads.

\subsection{Competing Methods}
The comparison set is taken directly from \texttt{gnn\_main.py}. It spans simple heuristics, population-based metaheuristics, and prior graph-learning baselines: random assignment and a gain-per-area greedy heuristic as lightweight references; a CVXPY-backed MILP formulation as an exact small-graph reference; PSO and DBPSO as canonical swarm optimizers \cite{kennedy1995particle,kennedy1997discrete}; CLPSO and CCPSO as more advanced particle-swarm variants \cite{liang2006comprehensive,li2011cooperatively}; ESA, SHADE, JADE, and GL25 as representative simulated-annealing, differential-evolution, and genetic-algorithm baselines \cite{siarry1997enhanced,tanabe2013success,zhang2009jade,garcia2008global}; and \gcps as a prior GNN-based HW/SW partitioning approach \cite{zheng2021hardware}. We also include the placement-only \diffgnn model as an ablation to isolate the contribution of the proposed ordering head.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}
For every repaired partition, we compute three makespan metrics:
\begin{equation}
M_{\mathrm{DAG}}, \qquad M_{\mathrm{static}}, \qquad M_{\mathrm{learned}}.
\end{equation}
$M_{\mathrm{DAG}}$ is the baseline DAG makespan. $M_{\mathrm{static}}$ is the makespan obtained by LSSP with static priorities derived from the partition. $M_{\mathrm{learned}}$ is the LSSP makespan obtained by using the learned software-priority scores; it is only available for \diffgnnorder.

The reported metric is
\begin{equation}
M_{\mathrm{report}}=
\begin{cases}
M_{\mathrm{static}}, & \text{for all non-order-aware methods, including \diffgnn,}\\
\min(M_{\mathrm{static}},M_{\mathrm{learned}}), & \text{for \diffgnnorder.}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
We still list $M_{\mathrm{DAG}}$ in every table as a baseline for comparison.

\subsection{Synthetic Scaling Benchmark}
To study scalability beyond the real TOSA graphs, we add a SqueezeNet-like synthetic generator to the codebase. The generator uses \texttt{squeeze\_net\_tosa.dot} as the structural template, extracts its topological-layer profile and inter-layer edge pattern, and then produces a larger DAG with the same edge-to-node ratio:
\begin{equation}
\frac{|\gE_{\mathrm{syn}}|}{|\gV_{\mathrm{syn}}|}
\approx
\frac{234}{179}.
\end{equation}
For a requested node count $N$, the target edge count is
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{syn}} = \mathrm{round}\!\left(\frac{234}{179}N\right).
\end{equation}
In this paper, we use $N\in\{11,50,1000,10000\}$. After generating the synthetic topology, node and edge costs are sampled with the same $(k,l,\mu,\alpha)$ cost model as on the real graphs.

\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:results}
This section reports an initial single-run pilot used to populate the current draft. The full multi-seed campaign is still in progress, so the numbers below should be interpreted as preliminary sanity-check results rather than the final averages of the paper. For these pilot runs, we keep the evaluation protocol fixed, but reduce the per-method runtime budgets so that all baselines can be executed once on the three requested graph families. Following Sec.~\ref{sec:settings}, every non-order-aware method is reported by its static-priority makespan, while \diffgnnorder is reported by
\begin{equation}
M_{\mathrm{report}}=\min(M_{\mathrm{static}},M_{\mathrm{learned}}).
\end{equation}
We do not include the MILP baseline in the SqueezeNet-TOSA and synthetic pilot comparisons because those graphs are intended to probe the regime where exact optimization is no longer practical.

The small 11-node graph already shows the expected value of ordering-aware decoding. The placement-only \diffgnn run reaches $M_{\mathrm{static}}=31$, while \diffgnnorder reduces the learned-priority makespan to $29$, matching the best metaheuristic pilot results (DBPSO, SHADE, JADE, and GL25). On this graph, the learned ordering head is therefore directly responsible for the two-cycle improvement from $31$ to $29$.

The larger pilot results are more mixed. On the single SqueezeNet-TOSA run, Greedy and PSO both obtain the lowest pilot static-priority makespan of $8672$, while \diffgnnorder is close at $8704$. The learned ordering head does not improve this particular run because $M_{\mathrm{learned}}=8877 > M_{\mathrm{static}}=8704$, so the reported result falls back to the static schedule. On the 50-node synthetic graph, the placement-only \diffgnn pilot gives the best reported makespan of $1490$, clearly ahead of the non-learning baselines, whereas \diffgnnorder reaches $2075$. This indicates that the current short-budget order model is not yet tuned for the synthetic regime, and that the final large-scale study should be based on the full training budgets and multi-seed averages rather than the current pilot alone.
