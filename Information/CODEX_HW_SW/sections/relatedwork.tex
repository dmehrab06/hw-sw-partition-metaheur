\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\subsection{Exact and Metaheuristic HW/SW Partitioning}
HW/SW partitioning has traditionally been studied as a constrained combinatorial optimization problem. Surveys by Arat\'o \textit{et al.}, Hou \textit{et al.}, and Teich describe a broad range of exact approaches, including dynamic programming, integer programming, and branch-and-bound, together with heuristic and metaheuristic alternatives \cite{arato2005algorithmic,hou2019survey,teich2012hardware}. Exact optimization remains valuable for small instances, but runtime degrades rapidly as the DAG becomes larger and the evaluation function becomes more scheduling-aware. This is precisely why population-based methods such as PSO, binary PSO, CLPSO, cooperative coevolutionary PSO, differential-evolution variants, genetic algorithms, and simulated annealing remain attractive baselines \cite{kennedy1995particle,kennedy1997discrete,liang2006comprehensive,li2011cooperatively,tanabe2013success,zhang2009jade,garcia2008global,siarry1997enhanced}.

\subsection{Graph-Based Partitioning}
Graph neural networks are a natural fit for HW/SW partitioning because the input is already a graph and the output is a node-level assignment. Zheng \textit{et al.} proposed a graph-convolution-based HW/SW partitioner that combines graph features, graph convolution, and schedule feedback to improve solution quality on large task graphs \cite{zheng2021hardware}. Their method motivates the present work in two ways. First, graph features such as hardware gain and gain per unit area are directly useful for partitioning. Second, the schedule should not be treated as a purely downstream concern. The repository implementation studied in this paper builds on both observations and pushes the second one further by explicitly learning a software execution order inside the optimization loop.

\subsection{Differentiable Ordering}
The key methodological difference in our work is the use of a differentiable ordering head together with Sinkhorn-based soft permutations and a differentiable resource-order surrogate. These tools connect the method to differentiable ranking and latent-permutation models \cite{mena2018gumbel}. In the current paper, however, their purpose is not generic ranking; rather, they are used to approximate the single-processor software order induced by a candidate HW/SW partition. This schedule-aware emphasis is what distinguishes \diffgnnorder from placement-only graph partitioners and from population-based black-box baselines.
